Our standards

Good for people. Respectful of place.

Our nature-based work is guided by practical risk planning, participant choice, accessible facilitation and care for the living environment.

Safety & participant wellbeing

Ten areas we consider when planning sessions.

This public framework sets out our intended approach to responsible delivery. Exact controls depend on the participants, materials, location and agreed programme.

01 / 10

Programme suitability

We first consider participant age, interests, abilities, familiarity with green spaces, learning or wellbeing aims, and whether the activity is suitable for the setting.

02 / 10

Health, allergies & sensitivities

We ask organisers to flag relevant plant, pollen, sap, latex, insect, soil, fragrance and other sensitivities. Accommodations depend on the specific risk and can include observation-only or substitute materials.

03 / 10

Plant and material selection

Prepared materials are checked for suitability. We avoid unknown, toxic, irritant, thorny or otherwise unsafe plants and discourage eating or tasting plant material unless separately assessed and supervised.

04 / 10

Choice, consent & boundaries

We explain activities in an accessible way and respect a participant's decision not to touch, smell, handle or join a task. Additional assistance is offered with consent.

05 / 10

Environmental care

Activities follow site rules, minimise damage to habitats and avoid collecting protected plants, disturbing wildlife or introducing unsuitable species. Natural materials are prepared or sourced appropriately.

06 / 10

Site, weather & air quality

Before outdoor work, we consider paths, terrain, shade, hydration, toilets, heat, rain, lightning and current air quality advisories. Programmes can be adapted, moved indoors or stopped when conditions are unsuitable.

07 / 10

Access and inclusive participation

We consider mobility, sensory preferences, communication methods, seating, rest opportunities and the need for support staff. Activity intensity and materials are adapted where feasible.

08 / 10

Tools, soil & hygiene

We brief participants on handling tools, sharp objects, soil, water and plant materials. Handwashing, surface cleaning and safe storage are included where relevant.

09 / 10

Facilitator preparation

Facilitators should understand the aims, activity boundaries, selected plants, emergency arrangements and required support. Specialist or clinical work requires competent practitioners within their scope.

10 / 10

Monitoring and review

We observe how participants engage, pause when discomfort arises, record relevant incidents through the organiser's agreed process, and gather feedback where part of the programme.

Before we confirm a programme

What we need from organisers.

Early information lets us suggest safer and more suitable activities. Please flag relevant requirements in the planning discussion.

  • Number and age range of participants
  • Medical or allergy considerations relevant to plant activities
  • Access needs, mobility and sensory preferences
  • Space, venue and any site restrictions
  • Staff ratios, emergency contacts and supervision arrangements
  • Intended outcomes and whether specialist input is required

Important boundaries

We do not assume that all natural materials are harmless. Do not taste wild plants or fungi, touch unknown plants, collect protected flora, or enter restricted habitats during a programme.

Outdoor activities remain subject to weather, haze, venue regulations and the suitability of the group on the day.
