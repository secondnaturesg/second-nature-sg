Research & impact

Thoughtful programmes deserve thoughtful evaluation.

Not every activity needs to be a formal study. But well-chosen questions, observations and feedback can help us understand what participants experienced and how to improve.

🎯

Objectives & intended change

Clarify what the programme aims to support, from knowledge and engagement to self-reported mood and social participation.

📝

Practical feedback tools

Design simple forms, participant reflection prompts or observational notes suited to the group.

📊

Programme evaluation

Help partners review attendance, participation, satisfaction and changes reported by participants.

🤝

Research collaboration

Discuss possible pilot projects, research questions and ethical arrangements with education or care partners.

Being careful about impact claims

For example, Second Nature has reached more than 2,600 participants. Based on pre- and post-programme evaluations, 83% reported feeling more positive and 89.2% reported feeling less stressed after a session. These are self-reported findings and should not be interpreted as proof of a clinical effect without details of measurement, comparison groups and study design.

Where research involves vulnerable groups, personal information or publication, we discuss consent, data handling, partner approvals and ethics review as appropriate before collecting data.

We do not claim that a single post-session question proves a lasting improvement in mental health. The interpretation should match the measurement.
