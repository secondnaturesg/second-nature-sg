Nature Walks & Sensory Exploration

Walks with prompts for noticing local biodiversity, textures, sounds and relationships between species.

Nature-based programmes
Reconnecting with the living world

There is more to nature than meets the eye.

A nature walk can offer time to slow down, look closely and reconnect with the living world around us.

Through guided observation, sensory prompts and conversation, participants notice plants, animals and natural features that are easy to overlook in everyday life.

Walks can focus on biodiversity, nature-based science, sensory engagement, social connection or quiet reflection. Routes and activities are planned around each group.

What a session can include

01

Local biodiversity

Observe common plants, birds, insects and other wildlife, and consider how they use different habitats.

02

Sensory exploration

Notice sounds, colours, patterns, textures, changes in light and movement at a comfortable pace.

03

Relationships in nature

Look for interactions such as pollination, seed dispersal, shelter and feeding without disturbing living things.

04

Mindful observation

Pause for gentle noticing and reflection, with no pressure to speak or take part in every activity.

Examples of activities

Activities are selected and adjusted to suit the group and programme objectives.

  • Biodiversity spotting: Use simple prompts to notice observable characteristics of plants and wildlife.
  • Listening stops: Compare bird calls, insect sounds, wind and other sounds along the route.
  • Textures and patterns: Observe bark, leaf shapes, fallen seeds and natural surfaces; touch only where safe and permitted.
  • Microhabitats: Look for differences between shaded areas, open spaces and planted beds.
  • Species connections: Notice how plants offer food or shelter and how animals interact with their surroundings.
  • Nature reflection: Share one detail that stood out, or reflect privately if preferred.

What participants may gain

  • Notice and describe local biodiversity.
  • Practise observation, curiosity and asking questions.
  • Use sensory attention in a comfortable and respectful way.
  • Recognise simple relationships between plants, animals and habitats.
  • Spend time together in suitable green spaces.
  • Consider ways to care for natural areas.

Suitable for different groups

  • Children and primary students: Playful noticing, simple identification and age-appropriate science prompts.
  • Youths: Environmental observation, ecological relationships and shared discussion.
  • Adults and workplaces: Outdoor connection, gentle reflection and shared activity.
  • Seniors and care groups: Accessible routes, rest opportunities, familiar sensory experiences and conversation.
  • Mixed-age groups: Shared observations and exchanges between generations.

How we plan the session

A walk may combine short guided segments, stationary observation, breaks and optional group tasks. We review path conditions, shade, toilets and suitable stopping points in advance.

The pace and amount of guidance can change during the session. Some groups prefer more structured activities, while others benefit from extra time to observe.

What we need before the programme

To suggest a suitable route and session format, we will discuss the following details with organisers:

  • Group size and participant age range
  • Intended learning, engagement or wellbeing goals
  • Preferred location, date and duration
  • Mobility, access, sensory and communication needs
  • Relevant plant, insect or environmental sensitivities
  • Accompanying staff or caregiver support
  • Rest, toilet and sheltered facilities
  • Weather and air quality alternatives
Participation is by invitation, not pressure. Participants may observe rather than touch, take shorter parts of the walk or rest when needed. We respect individual choice and consider practical alternatives.

Caring for people and nature

We consider terrain, heat, weather, air quality, insect exposure, accessibility and the support available when preparing each route.

Participants are asked to stay on permitted paths, observe wildlife without disturbing it and follow park or nature reserve requirements. Unsuitable conditions may require route changes, an indoor alternative or postponement.

Programme scope

This is a nature-based education and wellbeing programme. It is not presented as psychological therapy or clinical treatment. Any specialist services require separate discussion and appropriate professional arrangements.
