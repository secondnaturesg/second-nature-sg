More than a walk in the park

Nature is not a backdrop. It is part of how we learn and connect.

A leaf can become a science lesson. A garden can offer an opportunity to practise everyday skills. A quiet moment beneath a tree can create space for reflection.

At Second Nature, we use plants, natural materials and the outdoors as a starting point for purposeful experiences. Sessions are adapted to participants' interests, abilities, comfort and learning goals.

  • Hands-on, inquiry-led learning
  • Choice, play and participant-led exploration
  • Nature connection, reflection and social participation
  • Accessible activities with thoughtful risk planning

The thinking behind our work

One natural world. Several ways to work with it.

Our programmes draw on ideas and practices from Forest School, forest therapy, nature-based interventions, therapeutic horticulture and recreational therapy.

These are related, but distinct fields. We use approaches that suit the group and its aims, without claiming every activity is clinical treatment or a formally accredited Forest School programme.

01

Follow curiosity

Offer prompts, choices and space for participants to ask questions, investigate and learn at their own pace.

02

Make it meaningful

Connect nature activities to practical skills, personal reflection and what matters to each group.

03

Care for people and place

Consider access, consent, safety, weather, plants and the living environment throughout the programme.

How we work

Designed with your group in mind.

01

Understand

We ask about your participants, goals, setting, access needs and comfort with nature.

02

Co-design

We suggest suitable activities, engagement methods, materials and a practical session flow.

03

Prepare & facilitate

We review plant and site risks, brief the team and facilitate with room for participant choice.

04

Reflect & improve

Where agreed, we gather feedback and observations to help review the programme.

People-first, nature-respectful

Your comfort matters. So does the living environment.

Plant sensitivities, allergies, insects, heat, accessibility and familiarity with outdoor spaces are part of our planning. Participation is by invitation, not pressure.
