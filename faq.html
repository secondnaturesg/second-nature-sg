Frequently asked questions

Good questions lead to better nature experiences.

From plant allergies and access to weather and programme planning, here are some things to consider.

Before you join or book

Your questions, answered.

Are programmes suitable for all ages?

We plan activities for children, youths, adults and seniors. Age appropriateness, group size and access needs are reviewed before confirming the format. Some activities or venues may not be suitable for every person.

What if a participant is allergic to plants or has sensitive skin?

Please share known allergies or sensitivities in advance, including reactions to pollen, sap, latex, soil, fragrances or insect bites. We can consider alternative materials, observation-only participation or a different activity. Where a risk cannot be managed suitably, the activity may not be appropriate.

What if someone is uncomfortable in forests or naturalised spaces?

That is important to know. We can consider shorter visits, predictable routes, a quieter pace, smaller group formats or suitable indoor activities. Participants are not pressured to touch plants or join every task.

Must the programme be held outdoors?

No. Many activities can be adapted for indoor rooms with suitable tables, materials, ventilation, hygiene access and facilitation space. Some outdoor experiences, such as ecosystem observation in place, cannot be replicated fully indoors.

What if it rains, there is lightning or haze?

We assess conditions using relevant weather and air quality advisories and the venue requirements. Plans may include an indoor option, modification, postponement or cancellation if outdoor delivery is unsuitable. Safety takes priority over completing the original activity.

Can we collect plants, leaves or twigs during a walk?

We do not encourage taking plants, flowers or other natural items from protected areas or where site rules prohibit collection. We use prepared, permitted materials or observation-based activities instead.

Do participants have to touch soil, leaves or insects?

No. Participants can observe rather than handle, and accessible alternatives may be possible. Any contact with materials should be explained and offered by choice.

Is this forest therapy or horticultural therapy?

Four fields inspire our activity design: Forest School, forest therapy, nature-based interventions and recreational therapy. Our team is certified in therapeutic horticulture. We will confirm whether a programme is an educational or wellbeing activity, therapeutic horticulture or another specialist service. A one-off workshop is not a certified Forest School programme or clinical treatment.

Can programmes support participants with additional needs?

Often, but suitability depends on the support needed, site and activity. We discuss access, communication, sensory needs and staff support in advance so the programme can be adapted where feasible.

What information do you need before a booking?

Tell us the preferred date or period, venue, participant number and age group, objectives, accessibility considerations, relevant plant or environmental sensitivities and whether you prefer an indoor or outdoor session.

Can we customise a programme?

Yes. We can plan around a theme such as science inquiry, mindfulness, social participation, gardening, intergenerational learning or a specific community outcome.

Do you offer consultancy, staff training and research support?

These are areas we can discuss with organisations. Scope, roles, deliverables and fees depend on the project and the professional expertise required.

Do you publish outcome measures?

We have reached more than 2,600 participants. Based on pre- and post-programme evaluations, 83% reported feeling more positive and 89.2% reported feeling less stressed after their session. These are self-reported programme findings, not clinical outcomes.

How much do sessions cost?

Fees depend on session format, length, materials, staffing, venue and participant numbers. Contact us with your brief for a quotation.
