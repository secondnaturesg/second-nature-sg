Intergenerational
Connecting generations

Different ages, one shared green space.

Nature activities can create shared experiences where people of different ages learn from one another and contribute together.

Our intergenerational programmes bring children, youths, adults and seniors into suitable activities that encourage interaction without forcing it. The focus is on shared participation, respectful communication and meaningful roles.

Sessions may involve guided observation, planting, making or group tasks. We consider the interests, abilities and support needs of all participants when planning activities.

What a session can include

01

Share knowledge

Make room for participants to exchange experiences and observations.

02

Work side by side

Use shared tasks with roles that can be adjusted for different ages and abilities.

03

Connect through nature

Notice plants, wildlife and green spaces together as starting points for conversation.

04

Contribute together

Create or care for something that the group can share with its community.

Examples of activities

Activities are selected and adjusted to suit the group and programme objectives.

  • Shared nature walk: Pair observation prompts with opportunities for conversation and rest.
  • Planting partners: Complete planting and watering tasks with shared roles.
  • Nature-memory sharing: Invite optional stories about plants, gardens and outdoor experiences.
  • Group artwork: Make a shared display using prepared natural materials.
  • Biodiversity observations: Compare what different participants notice in the same green space.
  • Community contribution: Create suitable items or displays for a shared centre space.

What participants may gain

  • Create opportunities for interaction across generations.
  • Recognise different skills and experiences within a group.
  • Support shared learning and cooperative activity.
  • Build confidence in communicating across age groups.
  • Encourage care for community spaces and nature.
  • Allow participants to contribute in personally meaningful ways.

Suitable for different groups

  • Schools with senior centres: Shared learning and simple paired tasks.
  • Youth volunteer groups: Supported activities with older adults and community participants.
  • Families: Nature-based tasks involving multiple generations.
  • Community organisations: Sessions built around shared participation and local spaces.

How we plan the session

Intergenerational programmes may be held in gardens, parks, community facilities or suitable indoor venues. Activities can be adapted to shorter segments with seating and rest opportunities.

Roles are discussed in advance so that no group is expected only to receive help. The plan aims to create opportunities for people to contribute in different ways.

What we need before the programme

For an intergenerational programme, early discussion should include:

  • Numbers and age ranges of each participant group
  • Aims for interaction and shared activity
  • Mobility, communication and sensory needs
  • Roles of teachers, caregivers and support staff
  • Venue, accessible routes, tables and seating
  • Any relevant health or material sensitivities
  • Whether participants know one another in advance
  • Intended shared output or closing activity
Participation is by invitation, not pressure. Participation should be respectful and voluntary. Facilitators create opportunities for interaction, but participants may choose how much to speak, share or join.

Caring for people and nature

We review supervision arrangements across age groups, accessible movement, consent, safe tool use and group size.

The programme is adapted to avoid putting responsibility for care or safety onto children or other participants who are not designated support staff.

Programme scope

This programme supports intergenerational learning and community participation. It is not a substitute for clinical or social care services.
