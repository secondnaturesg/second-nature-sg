Programme details

Nature-based experiences, made meaningful.

Our core activities bring people together through observation, sensory engagement, play, reflection and practical work with plants.

Curated nature-based programmes
Our core delivery

From a fallen leaf to a meaningful learning moment.

Second Nature sessions are designed around your group. We can bring nature to participants through garden-based work, walks, plant materials or structured activities in a suitable indoor setting.

What a session can include

🔬

Investigate & learn

Use observation, prediction and simple experiments to understand plants, water, light and habitats.

👣

Move & notice

Use accessible walks or stationary activities to explore sight, sound, scent and texture without pressure.

🪴

Care & grow

Pot, water, sort seeds, check plants and practise the steps involved in a meaningful task.

🫱

Connect & reflect

Use prompts, conversation and group tasks to build connection to self, others and the environment.

Examples of learning opportunities

  • Buoyancy: make predictions and compare how safe, prepared natural materials float or sink.
  • Light refraction: use clear water, light and safe materials to examine how an object appears to change when viewed through water.
  • Plant structure: compare leaves, roots and stems, noticing how different features help plants survive.
  • Local biodiversity: notice how plants, insects and other living things interact without taking or disturbing wildlife.
  • Attention and reflection: slow down, notice a detail and share thoughts or feelings if comfortable.
  • Daily living and participation: follow a sequence of gardening tasks that may involve reaching, grasping, measuring, pouring or watering.

What we will ask before the programme

The programme brief helps us determine what is suitable, rather than assuming every participant wants to be outdoors or touch plants.

  • Purpose of session, participant profile and intended outcomes
  • Age range, group size and support staff available
  • Allergies or sensitivities to plants, pollen, sap, soil, latex, insects or fragrances
  • Comfort and familiarity with naturalised environments
  • Mobility, sensory, communication or other access considerations
  • Location, weather alternatives, hygiene facilities and emergency arrangements
Choice matters. Participants may observe rather than touch, join a shorter segment, use alternatives to soil or opt out of an activity. The programme team agrees on feasible accommodations before delivery.

What we do not assume

Participating in a nature-based activity does not automatically make it a clinical therapy session. Where a client requires a treatment or rehabilitation service, the programme must be planned with an appropriately qualified professional and suitable goals, documentation and consent.
