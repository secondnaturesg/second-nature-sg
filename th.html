Therapeutic horticulture
Wellbeing through plant-related activity

Growing plants, practising meaningful skills.

Plant-related tasks can offer opportunities for meaningful participation, social connection and practice with everyday actions.

Our therapeutic horticulture activities may include seed sowing, potting, plant care, simple garden tasks and group projects. Tasks are chosen with each group's abilities, interests and goals in mind.

Our team includes practitioners certified in therapeutic horticulture. Activities can be adjusted for the pace, support requirements and environment of participants.

What a session can include

01

Plant care

Explore watering, checking plant condition and maintaining a simple care routine.

02

Hands-on tasks

Practise scooping, measuring, pouring, grasping and arranging materials through meaningful activity.

03

Shared growing

Work together on planting and maintenance tasks that support interaction and a sense of contribution.

04

Observation and reflection

Notice plant changes over time, recall experiences and reflect on the growing process.

Examples of activities

Activities are selected and adjusted to suit the group and programme objectives.

  • Seed sowing: Prepare containers, add growing medium, place seeds and label plants.
  • Potting and transplanting: Handle prepared plants and practise a simple sequence with suitable support.
  • Watering and measuring: Use appropriate containers to pour, measure and manage water.
  • Plant health checks: Observe leaves, stems, soil condition and visible changes.
  • Herb or sensory plants: Explore permitted scents and textures with attention to sensitivities.
  • Shared garden tasks: Plan and carry out small tasks that contribute to a centre's planted area.

What participants may gain

  • Take part in meaningful plant-related tasks.
  • Practise sequencing and everyday actions through activity.
  • Support attention and responsibility through care routines.
  • Encourage communication, choice and social engagement.
  • Observe plant growth and changes over time.
  • Contribute to a shared garden or planting project where feasible.

Suitable for different groups

  • Seniors and care groups: Supported gardening tasks, familiar routines and social interaction.
  • Adults and community groups: Plant care, shared growing and practical group tasks.
  • Youth groups: Learning about plants while practising responsibility and teamwork.
  • Mixed-ability groups: Adjusted materials, positioning and task steps based on support needs.

How we plan the session

Sessions may be delivered in a community garden, centre activity room or another space suitable for plants, soil and water. Indoor arrangements can be considered where accessible.

A programme may involve one session or repeated visits to support ongoing plant care. Task complexity, equipment and staff support are reviewed during planning.

What we need before the programme

To select suitable plants and tasks, organisers should share:

  • Group size, age range and participant interests
  • Intended participation or wellbeing goals
  • Mobility, grasp, seating and access needs
  • Relevant plant, soil, latex or fragrance sensitivities
  • Space, tables, water and cleanup facilities
  • Staff assistance and supervision arrangements
  • Whether plants can be maintained after the session
  • Any specialist goals or clinical requirements
Participation is by invitation, not pressure. Participants may complete selected steps, use adapted materials or observe. Assistance should support participant choice and independence where possible.

Caring for people and nature

We review plant selection, soil handling, sharp tools, water, hygiene, accessibility and safe storage of materials.

If specialist rehabilitation or clinical horticultural therapy is needed, scope, professional roles and assessment requirements must be agreed separately.

Programme scope

Our therapeutic horticulture activities support participation and wellbeing through plant-related tasks. They should not be assumed to be clinical horticultural therapy or a rehabilitation treatment plan.
