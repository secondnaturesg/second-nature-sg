Nature learning
Learning through questions

Small questions can lead to meaningful science.

Nature provides materials and observations that children and learners can use to ask questions, test ideas and compare results.

Our Nature Science Lab brings simple, hands-on investigation into school, community or suitable outdoor settings. Facilitators help participants make predictions, handle prepared materials and talk about what they notice.

Activities can connect to learning goals while leaving room for questions, trial and error, and different ways of expressing what has been learnt.

What a session can include

01

Ask and predict

Start with a question, make a simple prediction and decide what to observe or test.

02

Test with materials

Use prepared leaves, seeds, water and other safe items to explore simple science ideas.

03

Compare results

Notice similarities, differences and changes, and record findings in an age-appropriate way.

04

Explain and share

Use drawing, speech, demonstration or discussion to explain what happened.

Examples of activities

Activities are selected and adjusted to suit the group and programme objectives.

  • Floating and sinking: Compare how prepared natural materials behave when placed in water.
  • Light and refraction: Observe how an object looks through clear water using safe equipment.
  • Water movement: Investigate how water flows across different surfaces and materials.
  • Leaf and plant features: Compare shape, veins, surfaces and other visible plant features.
  • Decomposition: Look at prepared examples or observations of how organic materials change over time.
  • Mini-habitat study: Observe light, moisture and living things in a suitable garden or green space.

What participants may gain

  • Practise making predictions and checking them against observations.
  • Use scientific words appropriate to the learners.
  • Compare, sort and describe simple natural phenomena.
  • Work together while handling materials responsibly.
  • Develop curiosity and confidence in asking questions.
  • Relate observations to local nature and everyday settings.

Suitable for different groups

  • Preschool groups: Sensory sorting, simple cause-and-effect activities and guided observation.
  • Primary students: Structured investigation, simple recording and links to science topics.
  • Youth groups: More open-ended questions, comparisons and group explanations.
  • Mixed-ability groups: Shorter tasks, visual prompts and different ways to communicate findings.

How we plan the session

Sessions can be held indoors with tables and prepared resources, or outdoors where the setting supports safe observation and experimentation.

We select activities based on age, learning objectives, time available and space. Some investigations may be demonstrated, while others can be completed in small groups.

What we need before the programme

To prepare suitable science tasks and materials, we will ask organisers about:

  • Number and age range of learners
  • Relevant science topics or desired outcomes
  • Available tables, water access and working space
  • Group size and supervision arrangements
  • Relevant allergies and handling sensitivities
  • Communication or access support needed
  • Indoor or outdoor venue details
  • Time available and any cleanup arrangements
Participation is by invitation, not pressure. Learners may observe, draw, discuss or assist with selected steps instead of handling every material. Activities are adjusted to age and comfort.

Caring for people and nature

We use prepared materials and review water handling, slips, hygiene, tool use and cleanup needs before the session.

Experiments do not involve tasting unknown natural materials, collecting protected plants or disturbing wildlife.

Programme scope

This programme supports nature-based science learning. It is not a laboratory certification course, clinical service or formal research study.
