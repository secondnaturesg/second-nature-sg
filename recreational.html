Play, Make & Connect

Use twigs, fallen leaves and natural materials for creative work, group play and social interaction.

Recreation & community
Creating together

Simple materials, shared experiences.

Making and playing with natural materials can give people a reason to work together, try something new and share ideas.

Activities may include nature-based crafts, pattern-making, collaborative construction and simple group challenges. We select tasks based on participant needs and the setting rather than expecting everyone to make the same item.

The emphasis is on enjoyment, choice and participation, with opportunities for conversation, creativity and contribution.

What a session can include

01

Make with nature

Use prepared or permitted natural materials for simple creative projects.

02

Play and experiment

Try open-ended challenges that encourage curiosity and flexible thinking.

03

Work together

Share tools, make decisions and contribute to a group result.

04

Reflect and share

Talk about what was made, what worked and how it felt to participate.

Examples of activities

Activities are selected and adjusted to suit the group and programme objectives.

  • Nature patterns: Arrange prepared leaves, seeds and twigs into shapes, colours or repeating designs.
  • Group artwork: Create a shared collage or display using suitable natural materials.
  • Mini construction: Build simple free-standing forms with prepared materials.
  • Sorting and matching: Group items by texture, size, shape or observable features.
  • Creative prompts: Respond to a theme with an object, small display or collaborative activity.
  • Shared showcase: Present finished work to the group or centre if participants wish.

What participants may gain

  • Engage in recreation and creative expression.
  • Practise teamwork, communication and decision-making.
  • Use hands-on activity for social connection.
  • Explore natural materials in suitable ways.
  • Support participation with different levels of ability.
  • Create a shared item or group memory.

Suitable for different groups

  • Children and youth: Open-ended play, making, sharing and group challenges.
  • Adults and workplaces: Collaborative making and informal social connection.
  • Seniors and care groups: Accessible craft tasks, familiar materials and shared activity.
  • Intergenerational groups: Co-created work and exchanges between ages.

How we plan the session

Sessions can take place indoors or in suitable outdoor spaces. They may be more open-ended or structured, depending on the group's goals and comfort with materials.

Facilitators offer prompts and practical help while allowing participants to make choices about their own work.

What we need before the programme

To prepare the right materials and level of support, we will discuss:

  • Number and age range of participants
  • Interest in individual or group projects
  • Preferred venue and available table space
  • Mobility, vision, sensory or fine-motor needs
  • Relevant allergies or material sensitivities
  • Staff ratios and accompanying support
  • Time available and desired finished items
  • Material handling, hygiene and cleanup arrangements
Participation is by invitation, not pressure. The finished product is not the only measure of a good session. Participants can contribute in ways that are meaningful and comfortable for them.

Caring for people and nature

We use prepared or permitted materials and review sharp edges, small parts, tools, hygiene and potential allergens.

Natural materials should not be collected from protected areas or where site rules do not permit it.

Programme scope

This is a recreational and community programme informed by activity-based practices. It is not formal recreational therapy or clinical treatment.
