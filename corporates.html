Nature and workplace wellbeing

A different kind of break from the workday.

Nature at Work offers teams simple opportunities to pause, spend time together and pay attention to their surroundings.

Sessions can include guided outdoor walks, quiet sensory activities, hands-on plant tasks and optional reflection. The format is suited to teams that want an accessible experience rather than a long lecture.

We plan around group size, available time, location and what the organisation hopes staff will gain from taking part.

What a session can include

01

Pause and notice

Use simple attention prompts during a short walk or stationary activity.

02

Connect as a team

Share informal group tasks and conversations outside usual work routines.

03

Try plant-based making

Use suitable materials for a brief, hands-on nature activity.

04

Reflect on experience

Offer optional prompts for personal or team reflection.

Examples of activities

Activities are selected and adjusted to suit the group and programme objectives.

  • Guided park walk: Notice local plants, wildlife and natural features near the workplace.
  • Sensory pause: Listen, observe or spend a short time attending to the present moment.
  • Small-group challenge: Complete a simple nature observation or making task together.
  • Desk-friendly plant activity: Try a suitable plant-based task in an indoor setting.
  • Shared reflection: Discuss what stood out and how participants experienced the activity.
  • Closing takeaway: Identify one simple way to make room for nature in everyday routines.

What participants may gain

  • Offer staff a planned pause from routine work.
  • Encourage nature connection and present-moment attention.
  • Create opportunities for informal team interaction.
  • Support shared participation without specialist outdoor experience.
  • Introduce simple ways of spending time with nature.

Suitable for different groups

  • Corporate teams: Suitable for team gatherings, wellbeing events and staff activities.
  • Public and community organisations: Small-group engagement and reflection.
  • Mixed-experience teams: Activities with optional levels of participation.
  • Multi-generational workplaces: Formats suited to varied ages and comfort levels.

How we plan the session

Activities may be held in a nearby park, workplace garden or suitable indoor room. Session length and complexity are agreed based on the organisation's schedule.

We can use a more guided format or leave space for informal conversation. Outdoor plans require practical weather alternatives.

What we need before the programme

To propose a suitable team session, we will ask about:

  • Number of staff and broad participant profile
  • Purpose of the event and desired outcomes
  • Preferred date, time and duration
  • Office, park or venue location
  • Mobility, access and sensory requirements
  • Any plant or outdoor sensitivities
  • Space, equipment and indoor backup options
  • Whether the activity is part of a wider event
Participation is by invitation, not pressure. No one is required to share personal experiences, take part in mindfulness exercises or handle materials. Optional formats are available.

Caring for people and nature

We review weather, heat, accessibility, allergies and site rules for outdoor sessions, and safe material handling for indoor activities.

The activity is intended as a community or workplace wellbeing experience, not a medical or psychological service.

Programme scope

Nature at Work is a workplace wellbeing and engagement programme. It does not constitute therapy, employee counselling or a clinical intervention.
